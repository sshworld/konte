# konte

konte turns a short JSON spec into a storyboard sheet (panels with camera, action and dialogue notes, like a film storyboard) and a rough animatic, quickly enough to iterate on: the board takes a few seconds, and the video scales with its length and the machine. Nothing is generated by a model: a fixed renderer draws grey sets, one coloured mannequin per character, and the camera you described. The point is to see whether the idea works (who is where, what the camera does, how long each beat lasts) before spending money on real generation.

Run it as `konte` if that command exists. If it does not, use `node "${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/cli.mjs"` (keep the quotes: the path may contain spaces) in place of `konte` in every command below. It needs Node 22+, Chrome or Chromium, and ffmpeg for video. If Chrome is not found, set `CHROME` to its executable.

## The loop

1. **Write the spec.** Read `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/docs/spec.md` once, then write `<name>.json`. Start from the closest file in `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/examples/`. Keep it small: 3 to 7 scenes, one or two sentences of `note` per scene, and `dialogue` where someone speaks. Write titles, notes and dialogue in English unless the person asks for another language; the board shows them exactly as written.
2. **Lint it.** `konte lint <name>.json`. Fix every `error` and every `warn` you did not intend. Lint runs on the same staging model the renderer draws, so trust it over your own estimate of the geometry; it samples and simplifies, so it does not replace reading the board.
3. **Draw the board first.** `konte render <name>.json --board-only` is quick (a second or two for a few scenes) and writes only the board. Read `board-3d.png`. Check each shot against its Action line: is the action visible in the panels, is the subject the right size, would the event read without the words?
4. **Revise** the spec and repeat 2 and 3 until the board tells the story.
5. **Render the animatic.** `konte render <name>.json`. Show the person the board and `sketch-3d.mp4`, and say what you are unsure about. If they are going to a video model next, point them to `prompts.md` and the clips in `shots/`. A full render removes clips and prompts left in that folder by an earlier render.

Do not skip step 3. A board is nearly free; a wrong video costs the person's attention.

## Writing a spec that reads

- **One idea per scene.** If the note needs "and then", split the scene.
- **Describe the shot, not the camera position.** Use `size`, `angle`, `height` and `move`. Reach for a raw camera only when those cannot say it.
- **Give each character one strong colour** and keep it for the whole piece. Colour is how people are told apart across cuts.
- **Make speeds real.** Walking is about 1.4 m/s, running 3 to 8 m/s. A 16-second sprint covers about 100 m, so the set has to be that long.
- **A jump lasts under a second.** Use `"jump": [t0, t1, height]` with the `leap` action over the same time. For slow motion, give the jump its own short scene.
- **Close-ups need `part`.** `"size": "CU", "part": "hands"` aims at the hands. Without `part` a close-up aims at the head.
- **A camera that stays put loses a moving subject.** Use `track` or `pan` when someone travels.
- **Dialogue:** seat people with `sit`, add `"look": "<other>"`, use `"sit+talk"` for the speaker, and cut between `{"on": "a", "over": "b"}` and `{"on": "b", "over": "a", "side": "left"}`.
- **Showing a product:** make it a cast object with `"heldBy": "<person>"`, give the person `"stand+show"`, and put the camera `"on"` the object for the close-up.
- **Give a screen, sign or logo board its own `color`**, or it disappears into a grey wall. Its words go in the note, not the picture.
- **Dialogue goes in `dialogue`**, as `[["<cast id>", "line"]]`. It shows on the board and in both prompts, never in the video. Pair it with a `talk` gesture if the speaker should move.
- **Set pieces are for geography**, not decoration: the wall they run along, the gap they jump, the table between them. Three to eight pieces are usually enough.
- Use `beats` when the moment that matters is not the one konte picked for the board.
- **Keep scenes short** when the clips will feed a video model: 5 to 15 seconds, one scene per shot. Seedance 2.0 documents 15 seconds of reference video in total and Kling 4.0 3 to 30 seconds per clip.

## What to tell the person

- The board and video are a sketch of staging and timing. Faces, lighting, texture and style are not in it and are still theirs to decide.
- The video never has words on it: no titles, captions or name tags. Every word (shot numbers, camera, action, dialogue, who is which colour) is on the board and in `prompts.md`. `--review` writes an extra copy with travel lines on the floor, still without words.
- `shots/` holds one clip per scene plus its first and last frame, and `prompts.md` has two prompts per shot. konte's clips and prompts have not been tested with any video model yet: say so, and present them as a starting point built from the models' documentation. With a model that takes a reference video (Seedance 2.5 or 2.0, Kling 4.0), attach the clip and use prompt A. With an image-to-video model that takes no reference video (Seedance 1.5 Pro), the model may keep the grey sketch look of its first frame, so suggest restyling the still into a finished image first and then using it as the first frame with prompt B. Ask which model they use before telling them which files to take; limits differ by model and platform.
- Say plainly what the sketch cannot show (a facial expression, a product detail) rather than implying it is covered.
